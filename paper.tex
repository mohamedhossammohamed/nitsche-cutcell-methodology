\begin{abstract}
Symmetric Nitsche methods impose Dirichlet conditions on unfitted meshes
through a penalty $\lambda_T$ whose classical volume-scaled choice,
$\lambda_T=C_k\rho(T)/h_T$ with $\rho(T)=|T\cap\partial\Omega|/|T\cap\Omega|$,
diverges when the physical domain occupies a vanishing fraction of a background
element (a \emph{sliver cut}), inflating stiffness-matrix condition numbers and
destroying accuracy through round-off. This work contributes three things.
First, an exact-geometry computational framework in which circle cuts are
clipped in closed form via Green's theorem --- carrying no tessellation error at
any cut fraction --- ellipses reduce affinely to the same exact path, and only
genuinely non-quadric level sets fall back to adaptive subdivision with
per-cell \emph{measured} quadrature error. Second, a preregistered protocol
(formulas F1--F8, benchmark tiers T1--T5, numeric falsification gates,
Bonferroni-corrected statistics) frozen before any sweep data exists, designed
so that both positive and negative outcomes about bounded-$\lambda$
stabilization are publishable results. Third, a fully verified implementation:
33 passing checks including closed-form circular-segment identities down to cut
fraction ${\sim}5\times10^{-7}$, agreement of moments against an independent
polygon-clipping oracle, and a measured benign-cut validation in which the
manufactured-solution errors converge at observed rates
$p_{L^2}=1.85$ ($R^2=0.995$) and $p_{H^1}=0.97$ ($R^2=0.997$) for $P_1$, and
$p_{L^2}=3.17$ ($R^2=0.9998$) and $p_{H^1}=2.08$ ($R^2=0.9998$) for $P_2$,
with coercive spectra throughout. The preregistered controlled-sliver sweep and
the hypothesis gates H1--H3 are specified in full but \emph{not yet executed};
this manuscript reports no sliver-tier numbers because none have been measured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ be embedded in a background domain $D$ with a
mesh $\mathcal{T}_h$ independent of $\partial\Omega$. For the Poisson problem
$-\Delta u=f$ in $\Omega$, $u=g$ on $\Gamma=\partial\Omega$, the symmetric
Nitsche form seeks $u_h\in V_h$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nitsche}
a_h(u_h,v_h)=\sum_{T}\int_{T\cap\Omega}\nabla u_h\!\cdot\!\nabla v_h
-\sum_T\int_{\Gamma_T}\!\big[(\partial_n u_h)v_h+u_h(\partial_n v_h)\big]
+\sum_T \lambda_T\!\int_{\Gamma_T}\!u_hv_h
=\sum_T\int_{T\cap\Omega}fv_h+\int_{\Gamma_T}\!g(\lambda_Tv_h-\partial_nv_h),
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma_T=T\cap\Gamma$. Discrete coercivity requires, through local
trace--inverse estimates, a penalty scaling like
$\lambda_T\ge C\,\rho(T)\,h_T^{-1}$ with
$\rho(T)=|\Gamma_T|/|T\cap\Omega|$~\citep{cui2025multigrid}. Under a sliver
cut, $|T\cap\Omega|\to0$ while $|\Gamma_T|$ stays bounded below, so
$\rho(T)$ and hence $\lambda_T$ diverge: the condition number inflates, and
round-off swamps discretization error long before asymptotic regimes are
reached. Established remedies modify the \emph{space}: cell aggregation
constructs extended finite element spaces whose condition number provably
matches body-fitted behaviour~\citep{badia2019aggregated}, and related
agglomeration ideas appear across method families~\citep{rangarajan2025cutvem};
alternatively, stabilization terms such as ghost penalties or polynomial
extensions restore cut-position independence without changing the
space~\citep{burman2023unfitted,zheng2026mixed}, and solver-side treatments
attack the conditioning downstream~\citep{cui2025multigrid,voet2026deflation}.

The open question this study addresses, fixed in advance and verbatim, is:
does there exist a \emph{closed-form} stabilization parameter
$\Phi(\rho,h_T,k,\kappa,a_T)$ that (a) remains bounded in the sliver limit,
(b) preserves discrete coercivity independent of the cut fraction $\varepsilon$
and mesh size, (c) preserves the optimal rate $O(h^k)$, and (d) restores the
standard conditioning rate $\kappa(A_h)=O(h^{-2})$ --- and does it compete
with aggregation and ghost penalties on identical benchmarks?

Two properties distinguish this work from prior numerical studies of that
question. First, the geometric quantities entering any candidate formula ---
$\rho(T)$, curvature $\kappa$, aspect ratio $a_T$ --- are computed
\emph{exactly} for the circle family rather than approximated, eliminating
quadrature contamination of the conditioning signal at the very cut fractions
under study (\S\ref{sec:method}). Second, the entire experimental protocol was
frozen and hashed before a single sweep datum existed
(\S\ref{sec:protocol}): candidate list, grids, seeds, falsification gates, and
statistical corrections cannot drift after seeing data. At the time of writing
the implementation has passed its full verification suite and the
preregistered benign-cut validation has been measured and is reported here
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}); the controlled-sliver tier and hypothesis gates are
specified but not yet run, and we state that plainly rather than implying more.

Our contributions are:
(i) an exact-geometry cut-cell engine with machine-precision areas, boundary
measures, and curved-boundary quadrature for circular arcs at arbitrary sliver
fractions;
(ii) a degree-exact collapsed-coordinate simplex rule and a P1/P2 unfitted
symmetric Nitsche assembler with active-space restriction;
(iii) a hashed preregistration of the complete formula search with numeric
falsification criteria;
(iv) measured benign-cut validation achieving optimal rates for both degrees;
(v) an account of defect classes caught by independent-oracle verification,
several of which produce silently wrong numerics that mass-based sanity checks
do not detect.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Exact clipping of circle cuts}

For a disk boundary $\phi(x)=r^2-|x-c|^2>0$ inside, the region
$T\cap\Omega$ within a triangle decomposes into polygonal runs along
$\partial T$ plus circular-segment crescents. Areas follow in closed form from
Green's theorem: each directed line piece contributes
$\tfrac12(a_xb_y-b_xa_y)$, and an arc swept from angle $a_0$ to $a_1$ about
centre $c$ contributes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:greenarc}
\tfrac{R}{2}\Big[c_x(\sin a_1-\sin a_0)-c_y(\cos a_1-\cos a_0)+R\,(a_1-a_0)\Big],
\end{equation}
with signed sweep taken in the traversal direction. Boundary measures
$|\Gamma_T|$ are arc lengths, likewise exact. Interior integrals use a Gauss
rule on the polygonal fan plus tensor Gauss strips over each crescent in a
$(\theta,\rho)$ frame bounded below by the chord; angular panels exist purely
for quadrature order, so combined mass reproduces the closed-form area to
relative $10^{-13}$ while every basis-product integral converges
exponentially. Because a circle may cross a convex element up to six times,
the loop construction pairs every exit crossing with the next entering
crossing counter-clockwise and asserts all boundary arcs subtend less than
$\pi$.

Ellipse cuts ride the same exact path: the affine map $y=D^{-1}(x-c)$ sends
the ellipse to the unit disk and the element to another triangle; weights pick
up $|\det D|$, normals pull back through $D^{-T}$, and the physical boundary
measure integrates directly against elliptic speed factors. Only genuinely
non-quadric shapes (e.g.\ superellipse ensembles) use adaptive midpoint
subdivision with tangent-line clipping of mixed leaves, terminated by a
curvature criterion; their accuracy is never assumed but re-measured by an
internal refinement self-check logged alongside results.

\subsection{Quadrature}

Simplex rules are generated from tensor Gauss--Legendre products via collapsed
coordinates, $(s,t)\mapsto(s,(1-s)t)$ on the reference triangle. The Jacobian
factor $(1-s)$ raises the outer integrand degree by one, so an order-$n$ rule
is exact through total degree $2n-2$ --- one order below the naive product
bound, a distinction our exactness tests verify monomial-by-monomial against
closed factorial forms.

\subsection{Assembly and the active space}

The P1/P2 assembler implements form~\eqref{eq:nitsche} with per-element
$\lambda_T=C_k\Psi/h_T$, $C_k=4(k+1)^2$. Basis functions carry analytic
gradients (verified against finite differences); degrees of freedom are
vertices, then edge midpoints offset by the vertex count. Elements wholly
outside $\Omega$ contribute nothing, and dofs supported wholly outside are
dropped: the linear system lives on the active space $V_h^\Omega$, which is
essential --- unrestricted systems possess exactly singular rows and columns.
Optional ghost-penalty terms
$\sigma_{GP}\sum_F h_F^{2k+1-d}\int_F[\partial_nu][\partial_nv]$ and
AGFE-style aggregation with static condensation are implemented behind flags
for the later hypothesis tiers.

\section{Preregistered protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

Before any sweep data existed, the following were frozen and committed
(SHA-256 in the title footnote): thirteen closed-form formula variants
F1--F6b (baseline $\rho$; hard clips; aspect-ratio clip; harmonic blends;
logarithmic damping; curvature-augmented), the aggregation hybrid F7
($\varepsilon_c=10^{-3}$), and the train-split-fitted family F8; benchmark
tiers T1 (controlled sliver, $\varepsilon$ from $0.5$ to $10^{-6}$ crossed
with five mesh sizes, two degrees, cap-height placement solved to $10^{-15}$),
T2 (200 seeded random realisations per mesh, frozen 70/30 split), T3
(curvature sweep), T4 (3D generalization with frozen coefficients), T5
(sign-changing coefficient); a three-solver measurement trio (sparse LU,
CG+Jacobi, CG+AMG); shift-invert Lanczos spectra with explicit
non-convergence flags; and numeric falsification gates H1--H3 with
Bonferroni-corrected statistics. Four deviations from the originating
methodology document are logged inside the preregistration itself (PETSc
unavailability, supplementary mass-normalized eigenvalue logging, deferred
dual-backend cross-check, and the subdivision-quadrature policy). Any protocol
change after data would constitute a new registration, never a silent edit.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Verification suite}

Thirty-three automated checks pass. The sliver family is validated against
closed-form circular segments: placing the chord at depth $d$ makes
$|T\cap\Omega|=\tfrac12(\theta-\sin\theta)$ and
$|\Gamma_T|=\theta$, $\theta=2\arccos d$, and the computed quantities match to
$10^{-12}$ relative down to $d=0.999999$ (cut fraction
${\sim}5\times10^{-7}$). Random cuts are checked against an independent
midpoint-shifted polygon-clipping oracle for area, first, and second moments;
the subdivision path is cross-validated against the exact path where they
overlap mathematically (superellipse exponent 2 is an ellipse).

This verification campaign earned its cost by catching defects that
mass-consistency checks alone do not reveal: quadrature nodes sampling a
square under triangle weights (masses right, moments wrong); a spurious factor
one-half from double-counting the reference-triangle measure; P2 edge-dof ids
colliding with vertex ids through a missing namespace offset; an absolute
boundary-classification tolerance that degrades as refinement shrinks
elements; and, notably, two defects inside the verification oracle itself (a
sign-flipped ray parameter and a transposed $\int xy$ shoelace coefficient),
caught by adjudicating against closed forms. We document these classes because
each produces plausible-looking output.

--- the circle grazes some cells --- which is precisely why raw
$\kappa(A_h)$ for $k=2$ is large and non-monotone between $n=32$ and

preregistration logs mass-normalized companions alongside the gate quantity.
Second, inactive-dof counts grow with refinement as expected from the
geometry of the exterior region.

\subsection{What is not yet measured}

No controlled-sliver, random-ensemble, curvature, sign-changing, or
three-dimensional results exist at the time of writing; tiers T1--T5 and the
falsification gates H1--H3 therefore appear in this manuscript only as
registered protocol. We decline to extrapolate how any bounded formula will
behave at $\varepsilon\le10^{-3}$: the point of the frozen gates is that the
data will speak first.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Aggregation-based cures modify the discrete space itself:
\citet{badia2019aggregated} construct aggregated unfitted spaces whose
condition number provably matches body-fitted behaviour regardless of cut
position, establishing the benchmark our hybrid arm must beat.
Element-agglomeration strategies extend beyond Lagrangian spaces, e.g.\
shape-agnostic agglomeration for virtual elements~\citep{rangarajan2025cutvem}.
Space-preserving stabilizations instead repair the bilinear form:
polynomial-extension reconstructions remove small-cut ill-conditioning in
unfitted hybrid high-order methods~\citep{burman2023unfitted}, and kernel
stabilization plus ghost penalties deliver cut-position-independent constants
in mixed extended virtual elements~\citep{zheng2026mixed}. Solver-oriented
work treats the symptom downstream: multiplicative vertex-patch smoothers with
ghost-penalty-aware transfer give a practical multigrid for Nitsche-discretized
CutFEM~\citep{cui2025multigrid}, and deflation preconditioning addresses
immersed-method conditioning directly~\citep{voet2026deflation}.
Geometry-conforming immersed spaces offer an orthogonal route that replaces
the cut-cell polynomial space entirely~\citep{lin2026geometry}. The present
study is complementary to all of these: it holds the space fixed, varies only
the closed-form penalty $\Phi$ under preregistered falsification gates, and
supplies an exact-geometry measurement bed in which the regressors entering
any $\Phi$ carry no quadrature error for the quadric family.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We built and verified the measurement instrument before using it, froze the
experiment before running it, and report exactly what has been measured:
optimal-rate, coercive benign-cut validation for P1 and P2 unfitted symmetric
Nitsche on an exact-geometry stack. The preregistered sliver sweep --- thirteen
formula variants, five cut fractions, five meshes, two degrees, three solvers,
and the H1--H3 gates --- remains to be executed; its outcome, positive or
negative, will be written against the same hash that stamps this protocol.
If no bounded closed-form formula survives its gates, that negative result,
with evidence and exact statistics, is the publishable answer.
